# Verify a supporting move with the cameras before it leads

Under ADR 0008 a settled supporting move took the lead from a current camera sighting at once, and the re-check of the camera the Person left was skipped. In practice a BLE tracker often drifts into a neighbouring room through a wall, so the tracker moved the Person to a room they never entered, and with no camera there nothing corrected it until the next trigger. Now a settled move against a current camera sighting is verified by the cameras first, and the camera keeps leading until the verification has ended. First comes the old camera check: the Person's last camera is asked whether they are still there, worded like a re-check, about the moved Persons only. A confirmation is a normal sighting, newer than the move, so the move is overruled for good: only a new supporting change starts another verification. On a miss, the move check of ADR 0008 asks every camera of the new area, if it has any. Only when no camera confirms the Person does the supporting tracker lead. A failed run counts as a miss, so a broken camera cannot pin the Person to the room they may have left. Neither check is corroborated (ADR 0009): the supporting tracker disagrees with the old camera by definition, and a floor boost for the room it says the Person left would make no sense. The old camera check ignores the cooldown but claims it, like a re-check; Persons last seen on the same camera share one run, and Persons who need no old camera check (no current sighting) go straight to the move check. A Person without reference images is not asked about, so their move leads unverified. While no camera sighting is current the supporting tracker leads as before, and a move then only gets the move check. A return to the area the supporting tracker last settled in is still no move, unless a current camera sighting places the Person elsewhere: then it is verified like any move. The verification lives at runtime only; a move that settled while Home Assistant was down leads unverified. The existing move check option turns the whole verification off, and with it the old behaviour returns: a settled move leads at once. Refines ADR 0008: the camera sighting stops leading after a settled move only once the cameras have not confirmed the Person, and the trade-off that a wrong move is not caught until the next trigger is reversed. The re-check skip of ADR 0008 stays, since by then the old camera has already missed. Cost: at most one AI call on the old camera per settled move per Person, plus the move check as before; the settle period limits this to one every two minutes per Person, and the delay of a real move grows by the few seconds of one AI call. Rejected alternatives: running the old camera check and the move check in parallel (the old camera usually confirms in the drift case, so asking it first saves the move check, and the delay is small next to the settle period); and a longer settle period (it delays real moves just as much and still never asks a camera).
